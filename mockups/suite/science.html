Distance from redshift

Modelled

The distance to a galaxy or quasar from its redshift depends on the cosmological model as much as on the measurement. This uses flat ΛCDM with the Planck 2018 parameters.

Comoving distance today–c/H₀ ∫ dz/E(z), E(z) = √(Ωm(1+z)³ + ΩΛ). Where the object is now.
Light-travel distance–c × lookback time. How far the light went.
Lookback time–1/H₀ ∫ dz/((1+z)E(z))
Age of the universe then–Age today minus the lookback time

Radiation is neglected, which matters only at very high redshift. Planck Collaboration 2020, A&A 641, A6 (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing+BAO). A local measurement of H₀ near 73 km/s/Mpc shortens every distance by about 7 %: try it.

Star luminosity

Derived

A star's power output from its radius and surface temperature, treating it as a black body: the Stefan–Boltzmann law in solar units.

Luminosity–L/L☉ = (R/R☉)² (T/5772 K)⁴. The solar value is the IAU 2015 nominal temperature.
Peak wavelength–Wien's law, λ = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m K / T
Where the peak falls–Visible light spans about 380–750 nm

The defaults are Betelgeuse. Its published radius ranges from about 640 to 1,000 solar radii depending on method, so its luminosity is uncertain by a factor of two or more; the tool shows the value only to three figures for that reason.

How bright is Betelgeuse, and why is it red?

Given: radius R = 764 R☉, surface temperature T = 3,600 K, Sun T☉ = 5,772 K, Wien's constant b = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m K.

1. At what wavelength does Betelgeuse's spectrum peak?

λmax = b / T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ / 3,600 = 8.05 × 10⁻⁷ m = 805 nm, just beyond the red end of the visible. The star emits more strongly at red wavelengths than at blue, so it looks orange-red.

2. How many times more luminous than the Sun is it?

L/L☉ = (R/R☉)² (T/T☉)⁴ = 764² × (3,600/5,772)⁴ = 583,700 × 0.1513 ≈ 88,300. The radius alone gives 583,700; the cooler surface cuts each square metre's output to 15 %. The radius is the weaker value: published estimates span about 640 to 1,000 R☉.
