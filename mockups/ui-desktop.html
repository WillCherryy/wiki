Reptile

Reptiles are tetrapod animals in the class Reptilia, comprising turtles, crocodilians, squamates and the tuatara. Living reptiles are cold-blooded and lay amniotic eggs.

The largest living reptile is the saltwater crocodile, which can exceed six metres. Reptiles first appeared in the Carboniferous period, more than 300 million years ago, and are the ancestors of birds.

Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower on the Champ de Mars in Paris, named after the engineer Gustave Eiffel, whose company designed and built it for the 1889 World's Fair.

Although it was criticised by some of France's leading artists when it was built, it has become a global cultural icon of France.
