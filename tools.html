Distance from redshift

Modelled

How far away a galaxy or quasar is depends on the cosmological model as much as on its measured redshift. This uses a flat ΛCDM universe with the Planck 2018 parameters.

Comoving distance today–c/H₀ ∫ dz/E(z), with E(z) = √(Ωm(1+z)³ + ΩΛ). Where the object is now.
Light-travel distance–c × lookback time: how far the light travelled
Lookback time–1/H₀ ∫ dz/((1+z)E(z))
Age of the universe then–Age today minus the lookback time

Radiation is neglected, which matters only at very high redshift. Parameters: Planck Collaboration 2020, A&A 641, A6 (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing+BAO). Local measurements give H₀ near 73 km/s/Mpc, which shortens every distance by about 7 %: try it.

Star luminosity

Derived

A star's power output from its radius and surface temperature, treating it as a black body: the Stefan–Boltzmann law in solar units.

Luminosity–L/L☉ = (R/R☉)² (T/5772 K)⁴, using the IAU 2015 nominal solar temperature
Peak wavelength–Wien's law, λ = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m K / T
Where the peak falls–Visible light spans about 380–750 nm

The defaults are Betelgeuse. Its published radius ranges from about 640 to 1,000 solar radii depending on the method, so its luminosity is uncertain by a factor of two or more. Results are given to three significant figures for that reason.

A level physicsStellar radiation

How bright is Betelgeuse, and why is it red?

Given: radius R = 764 R☉, surface temperature T = 3,600 K, the Sun's surface T☉ = 5,772 K, Wien's constant b = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m K.

1. At what wavelength does Betelgeuse's spectrum peak?

λmax = b / T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ / 3,600 = 8.05 × 10⁻⁷ m = 805 nm, just beyond the red end of the visible. Across the visible band the star emits more at red wavelengths than at blue, so it looks orange-red.

2. How many times more luminous than the Sun is it?

L/L☉ = (R/R☉)² (T/T☉)⁴ = 764² × (3,600 / 5,772)⁴ = 583,700 × 0.1513 ≈ 88,300. The radius alone would give 583,700; the cooler surface cuts each square metre's output to about 15 % of the Sun's.

3. Published radii for Betelgeuse range from about 640 to 1,000 R☉ at the same temperature. What range of luminosity follows?

L scales as R², so 0.1513 × 640² ≈ 62,000 and 0.1513 × 1,000² ≈ 151,000. A 56 % spread in radius becomes a factor of 2.4 in luminosity: the uncertainty in the input is squared, which is why the tool reports only three figures.
