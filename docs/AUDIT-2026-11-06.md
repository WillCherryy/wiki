# Scorcher Build: adversarial scientific and technical audit

6 November 2026. Scope: the technical paper (`mockups/suite/build-paper.html`), `earthbench/`, `gisbench/` (core operations only), `backend/` (the live app) and the results in `earthbench/results/`.

**Conflict of interest.** The auditor (Claude) wrote most of this code. To limit that, conclusions rest on calculations run during the audit, especially ones that do not reuse the code under test, not on recollection or on the paper's claims.

**What was run during the audit (6 November 2026, this environment):**
- `npm test`: all suites pass, including 37 backend tests and 124 Python tests.
- An independent implicit finite-difference radial-flow simulator, compared with the well-test engine.
- An energy-closure check on the synthetic solar weather.
- A negative-skin probe of the well-test engine.
- An effective-slope analysis of the mining precedence pattern, and a re-solve of 12 pit tasks with an exact-cone precedence.
- A count of restricted-file reads across every model run record.

**Not verified:** OpenQuake against the PEER benchmark values; any engine against field or real-project data; any reference that needs paywalled full text. These are marked where they arise.

---

## A. Executive verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Scientifically credible in its current form? | **Partly.** Most engines implement the right equations, and the well-test and GIS engines hold up against independent checks. But one pack (geothermal Horner) is built on circular, non-physical data. The mining key has a confirmed slope error that penalises correct work. One engine has a confirmed failure mode that its validation missed. |
| Technically rigorous? | **Uneven.** The software engineering is careful: tests, deterministic generation, a sandbox, hash chains. The scientific validation is mostly internal consistency, and the "34 of 43 verified" figure overstates it. |
| Does it benchmark engineering competence? | **No, not yet.** The engines inside the tools do the engineering. What remains for the agent is transcribing parameters, converting units, choosing documents and obeying policy. A small model is near the ceiling on correctness. |
| Maturity | **Early prototype**: a working test harness with a few sound engines. Not an engineering benchmark. |

**Overall: technically promising but inadequately validated (section G).**

## B. Discipline by discipline

Scores are judgements on a coarse scale, not measurements: ±1 is noise. "Realism" means how far the task resembles real engineering work.

### Mining (`earthbench/mining.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 5 · implementation 6 · validation 4 · realism 3

**Governing model**
- Block value is max(process, waste) (lines 91–96).
- The break-even mill cut-off is g_c = c_p / (r·P) (line 99). This is Lane's marginal (mill) cut-off; it is correct for material already in the pit.
- The ultimate pit is solved as a maximum-weight closure by min-cut (Picard 1976). That is the correct formulation, and equal to the optimum Lerchs–Grossmann (1965) finds.

**Confirmed error: the slope geometry**
- `precedence_offsets(slope_deg, levels=4)` (line 103) applies the slope cone only up to 4 benches. Deeper benches rely on chaining the pattern, which does not reproduce the cone.
- I traced the effective overall slope over the model's 10 benches:

| Stated slope | Effective slope, grid axis | Effective slope, diagonal |
|---|---|---|
| 38.66° | 45.0° | 42.3° |
| 45° | 45.0° | 46.7° |
| 50.19° | 56.3° | 57.9° |

- The key therefore permits walls up to about 7° steeper than the brief's geotechnical slope. By the pack's own definition, that is unsafe.
- Re-solving 12 main-suite pit tasks with every bench's cone explicit (an exact cone at block centres), the key's pit value is 0.3–0.9% higher. The tolerance is 0.5%, so **an agent that applies the stated slope correctly fails 7 of those 12 tasks.**
- The "unsafe pit" check, `closure_violations` (line 154), uses the same pattern, so it cannot detect the error. The test and the thing tested share the same mistake.

**Other concerns**
- Values are rounded to whole dollars, and capacities are divided by a scale factor for the integer max-flow (`ultimate_pit`, lines 123–151). The effect on near-ties was not quantified.
- Every task in a suite seed shares one block model (`make_model(seed)`, line 201). The 48 main tasks therefore come from **2 geological worlds**.

**Unverified:** no comparison with a published optimum, such as the MineLib instances (Espinoza et al. 2013).

**Most important fix:** use full-depth explicit cones, or a published precedence scheme with a stated slope error, and validate against MineLib known optima.

### Geothermal resource (`earthbench/geothermal.py`, `heat_in_place_mc`, lines 42–53)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 6 · implementation 7 · validation 4 · realism 3

**Governing model**
- H = A·h·[ρ_r·c_r·(1−φ) + ρ_f·c_f·φ]·(T − T_ref). This is the volumetric method (Muffler & Cataldi 1978), implemented correctly.
- Capacity: P_e = H·R·η / (L·F·t).
- P90 is the 10th percentile. That convention was verified in this audit by an analytic quantile check.

**Concerns (no confirmed errors)**
- **Fixed conversion efficiency.** η is fixed at 0.10–0.12, independent of temperature. In practice, conversion efficiency depends strongly on resource temperature; the USGS assessments of Williams et al. (2008) use a temperature-dependent utilization efficiency. Not verified in full text during this audit.
- **Independent inputs.** The Monte Carlo inputs are drawn independently; no correlations are modelled.
- **Arbitrary temperature distribution.** The triangular distribution uses the log's minimum, mean and maximum. That is a design choice with no statistical basis, and it makes the answer depend on gauge noise.

**Validation:** the Monte Carlo collapses to the closed form. That is a necessary check, not a sufficient one.

**Most important fix:** benchmark against a published USGS-style assessment with its inputs and outputs, and add temperature-dependent efficiency.

### Geothermal Horner well test (`earthbench/geothermal.py`, `_welltest_world`, lines 131–147)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 2 · implementation 5 · validation 1 · realism 1

**Confirmed: non-physical data and a circular key**
- The buildup data are generated from the Horner semilog equation itself (line 140).
- "Wellbore storage" is a multiplicative factor, 1 − exp(−Δt/0.35 h) (line 141). That is not the storage solution.
- "The fault" is an invented logarithmic steepening that starts at 25 h (line 143), not an image well.
- The key is a Horner fit to that same data over a window the brief gives the agent (line 273).
- So the task measures whether the agent can type a given window into a Horner tool, and the validation ("Horner recovers the true k within 3%") is circular.
- The landing page claims this pack tests "storage and faults outside the radial-flow window". That claim is not supported.

**Fix:** retire this pack, or regenerate it from `welltest_engine` with the window not given.

### Well tests, full (`earthbench/welltest_engine.py`, `earthbench/welltest.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 7 · implementation 6 · validation 6 · realism 4

**Governing model**
- Agarwal, Al-Hussainy & Ramey (1970) Laplace-space solution with storage and skin (`pwd_laplace`, line 36).
- Gaver–Stehfest inversion, N = 12 (Stehfest 1970), at line 43.
- An image well for the fault (line 65), superposition for the buildup, and the Bourdet (1989) derivative (line 91).
- Dimensionless groups and units are consistent.

**Independent check (audit-written finite-difference simulator, sharing no code or method with the engine)**
- With storage, it agrees to within 0.1–0.4% for C_D = 0 and C_D = 1000 with S = 5, over t_D = 10 to 10⁶. That is good independent evidence for zero and positive skin.

**Confirmed defect: negative skin**
- With negative skin, the engine returns non-physical values at early time: for S = −2, C_D = 300, p_D = −68,902 at t_D = 100.
- Against the standard effective-wellbore-radius treatment, r_wa = r_w·e^(−S) (Earlougher 1977), it is 4.7% off at t_D = 1,000 and converges only by t_D ≈ 10⁵.
- Cause: an infinitesimal-skin model with S < 0, amplified by Stehfest.
- **Impact:** 18 of the 48 public tasks have S < 0. A scan found none whose delivered data falls non-physically, because the earliest sample is already at t_D of about 10³ or more. The keys come from radial flow, where the engine is correct.
- So this is a latent defect, but the validation register marks the engine "verified" without ever testing S < 0.

**Concerns**
- The grading window rule (10% of radial flow, 25% slack in time) was tuned after the data-only scripted analyst first failed it. That is a researcher degree of freedom; it is disclosed in the code, but it is a choice made after looking.
- Homogeneous reservoir, single well, constant rate, no dual porosity, no rate history.

**Results:** Haiku passed 24/24, with k within 1%. The method is within a small model's reach once a derivative tool is supplied.

**Most important fixes:**
- Use the effective-radius treatment for S < 0 and add negative-skin tests.
- Validate against published type-curve values or a real field test.
- Remove the "how to read the plot" hints from the tool descriptions.

### Seismic hazard (`earthbench/hazard.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 5 · implementation 6 · validation 3 · realism 3

**Governing model:** classical PSHA (Cornell 1968) via OpenQuake hazardlib (Pagani et al. 2014). That is a correct engine choice.

**Concerns**
- **Site, rates and model don't match.** The site is fixed at Madrid (−3.70°, 40.40°; line 27), with M ≥ 4.5 rates of 0.05–0.4 per year within 15–70 km. That is high for central Iberia, plausibly by orders of magnitude. The California-dominated NGA-West2 model (Boore et al. 2014) is then applied as "active shallow crust". The world is internally coherent but geologically incoherent. Regional rates were not checked against a catalogue during this audit.
- **Basin depth.** `z1pt0` is fixed at 40 m for every Vs30 (line 64). Boore et al. (2014) include a basin-depth term, so fixing z1 for a soft site biases its motion. The size of the effect was not measured.
- **Simplified source model.** One vertical strike-slip nodal plane and no logic tree.
- **Arbitrary unsafe threshold.** The 10% underestimation threshold has no source.

**Validation:** metamorphic tests only, for example doubling the rates gives 1 − (1 − PoE)². The PEER verification benchmarks (Hale et al. 2018) have not been run. The register correctly marks this "source only".

**Fix:** run the PEER set 1 tests through the same configuration code, and use a real regional model or label the world as fictional.

### Solar (`earthbench/solar.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 6 · implementation 7 · validation 5 · realism 4

**Checked:**
- The weather year is internally consistent: GHI = DNI·cos z + DHI to within 0.06 W/m² (95th percentile) over 3,819 daylight hours.
- The solar-position routine reproduces the published NREL worked example.

**Concerns**
- **No incidence-angle (reflection) loss.** The pvlib chain (lines 55–60) omits it, and PVWatts v5 (Dobos 2014) includes one. This likely overstates energy by a few per cent. Not quantified in this audit.
- **Isotropic sky.** The Liu & Jordan model underestimates tilted-plane irradiance compared with Perez et al. (1990).
- **Unrealistic climate.** The synthetic climate's annual GHI is 1,496 kWh/m², with a daylight diffuse fraction of 0.52. That is cloudier than published values for Madrid; PVGIS was not queried during this audit.

**Most important fix:** compare against SAM or PVGIS for the same inputs at one real site, and add the incidence-angle loss.

### Wind (`earthbench/wind.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 5 · implementation 6 · validation 3 · realism 3

**Checked:** the formulas are correct: power-law shear, ρ = p/(R_d·T), and IEC (ρ/ρ₀)^(1/3) normalisation for pitch-regulated turbines.

**Concerns**
- **Idealised power curve.** C_p is constant at 0.45 below rated (line 32), unlike real curves.
- **Density at the wrong height.** It is computed at mast height (line 53), not corrected to hub height. Plausibly contrary to IEC 61400-12-1's measurement requirements; not checked against the standard's text.
- **Missing effects.** No wakes, no turbulence correction, no long-term correction, and no P90 uncertainty. Lenders rely on P90.

**Validation:** mostly self-consistency (`c_iec` at validation.py:194 compares `aep()` with itself; see section D). The Betz limit check is genuine physics.

**Fix:** use a certified manufacturer curve or the IEA 15 MW reference curve, and benchmark AEP against windpowerlib or a published site study.

### GIS (`gisbench/`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 7 · implementation 7 · validation 7 · realism 4

**Checked**
- Horn (1981) slope (`ops.py:55`).
- Area as cell count × cell area in a projected CRS (line 123). The projection's scale factor is ignored; the effect is about 0.1%, well inside tolerance.
- **The strongest validation in the repository:** an independent second engine (QGIS 3.34 and GDAL) agrees on all 180 tasks (IoU ≥ 0.996).

**Concern:** the traps are signposted. The paper concedes this.

### Seismic data (`earthbench/seismic.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 5 · implementation 6 · validation 4 · realism 2

**Checked:** SEG-Y header bytes are correct: the scalar at 71–72 applies to coordinates at 181–188, and the sample interval is at 117 (verified against segyio's field definitions).

**Concerns**
- **Trivial picking.** The picker takes the maximum in a window, and the brief gives the window and polarity convention.
- **Possibly contested polarity terminology.** "SEG normal = peak" for a zero-phase wavelet: usage varies, as Simm & White (2002) discuss. Not checked against the full text. The text header defines the convention, so the task is still unambiguous.

**Fix:** use real public data, such as the F3 Netherlands survey, with picks from more than one interpreter.

### Scheduling (`earthbench/scheduling.py`)
**Scores:** scientific correctness 6 · implementation 7 · validation 5 · realism 3

**Checked:** the CP-SAT formulation is correct for its model, and the optimum is proven.

**Concerns**
- The modelling choices have no source:
  - a move after every well, including the last;
  - maintenance blocking moves;
  - hours rounded up to days.
- The brute-force check shares every one of these assumptions. It validates the solver, not the model.

**Result:** Haiku passed 24/24. With an exact-solver tool, the task reduces to cleaning the inputs.

### Documents (`earthbench/documents.py`)
**Scores:** method 5 · implementation 6 · validation 3 · realism 2

**Concerns**
- **Tautological key.** The key is whatever the generator wrote.
- **A known gap in the provenance check.** It misses values from an original permit after an amendment changed them. The README discloses this.
- **Unrealistic documents.** Short templated text, with no PDFs, tables or scans.

**Result:** Haiku answered 48/48 correctly.

## C. Critical defects (by severity)

1. **The benchmark does not measure engineering competence.**
   - The engineering happens inside the tools (`heat_in_place_mc`, `pit_optimise`, `wind_aep`, `pv_yield`, `schedule_optimise`).
   - Briefs give the parameters, the windows and often the method. The traps are signposted in metadata.
   - Haiku correctness: 95/96 on geothermal families, 48/48 documents, 24/24 scheduling, 24/24 full well tests.
   - **Consequence:** high scores say little about competence, and the suite cannot separate strong agents.
   - **Fix:** tasks that need choosing a method, recognising that the data are insufficient (abstention), multiple dependent stages, unsignposted defects, and real data.
2. **Validation is overstated.**
   - "34 of 43 verified" mixes very different kinds of check:
     - tautologies, such as `c_cutoff` (validation.py:133), which re-derives the formula it tests;
     - self-comparison: `c_iec` (line 194) calls `aep()` on both sides;
     - library round-trips: `c_segy_scalar` (line 274) checks segyio rather than Scorcher;
     - genuine checks against external values.
   - Fewer than half compare against something outside the code (section D).
   - No engine has been compared with external reference data such as PEER values, MineLib optima, SAM or PVGIS outputs, or field tests. There is no expert review.
   - **Fix:** relabel the register by kind of evidence, and add the external comparisons.
3. **The mining slope error penalises correct work.** See section B (mining.py:103 and :154). **Fix:** full-depth cones; a MineLib comparison.
4. **The geothermal Horner pack is circular and non-physical** (geothermal.py:140–143, :273). **Fix:** retire it, or rebuild it on `welltest_engine` with no window given.
5. **The paper misreports the safety finding.**
   - The paper says the model "opened a confidential file" (build-paper.html:334).
   - All 17 recorded attempts were refused, with 0 content returned.
   - The earthbench environment lists the restricted file in `files_list` (core.py:219–220) and penalises opening it. That is closer to a curiosity trap than an access test.
   - The injection suite's leak test can never fire in earthbench, because restricted content is never returned.
   - **Fix:** correct the paper; stop listing restricted files (the backend already doesn't); add a realistic exfiltration path, such as a readable file containing a secret the policy forbids sending.
6. **The private held-out sets do not prevent training on the task distribution.**
   - Private tasks are samples from the public generator, with seeds from a space of 900,000 values (private.py:51).
   - Anyone can generate the whole distribution, so there is little to "keep private".
   - The commitment proves the set is unchanged; it does not prove it was unseen.
   - **Fix:** private tasks built from non-public material: real data, expert-authored briefs, or a generator not published.
7. **Well-test negative-skin failure.** welltest_engine.py:36–48. **Fix:** an effective wellbore radius for S < 0, plus tests.
8. **The statistics assume independence that does not hold.**
   - Mining's 48 main tasks come from 2 block models. Variants of one world are correlated.
   - Mostly one run per task, and one model. Wilson intervals and McNemar p-values are therefore too narrow or optimistic.
   - **Fix:** clustered or bootstrapped intervals by world; more worlds, fewer variants; repeated runs.
9. **Hazard configuration realism and validation.** The site and rate mismatch, fixed z1pt0, and no PEER run (hazard.py:27, :64). **Fix:** run PEER; use a real regional model.
10. **Integrity claims rely on trust in the repository holder.**
    - The trace and ledger hash chains are unsigned and unanchored (ledger.py:43, :64). Anyone with write access can rewrite and recompute them.
    - "Tamper-evident" holds only against partial edits.
    - **Fix:** sign entries, and anchor the ledger head externally, for example with an RFC 3161 time-stamp or in a third-party transparency log.

## D. Validation gaps

Kinds of evidence in the register:

| Kind | Entries |
|---|---|
| External published value | SPA worked example (as reproduced in pvlib's tests); SHA-256 FIPS vector; SAPM coefficients |
| Analytical solution | Stehfest against the Theis transform pair; storage, skin, fault doubling; Bourdet plateau; P90 quantile; Monte Carlo collapse; return period |
| Independent implementation | SciPy Wilson and McNemar; networkx min-cut; brute force (sharing the model's assumptions); QGIS (gisbench, outside the register) |
| Internal consistency or tautology | `c_cutoff`, `c_iec`, `c_isa` (a constant), `c_shear`, `c_ricker`, `c_units` (definitions), `c_chain`, horner-k (circular) |
| Expert review | none |
| External reference data | none |

**Needed before Scorcher calls itself a rigorous engineering benchmark:**
1. **Hazard:** PEER PSHA verification (Hale et al. 2018) through Scorcher's own configuration code.
2. **Mining:** MineLib ultimate-pit optima with stated slopes, after the cone fix.
3. **Well tests:** published type-curve values, or a documented field test with a known interpretation; negative-skin coverage.
4. **Solar:** a SAM or PVGIS comparison at a real site, with real TMY data.
5. **Wind:** a windpowerlib or published AEP comparison with a certified power curve.
6. **Geothermal resource:** reproduce a published USGS-style volumetric assessment.
7. **Seismic data:** real survey picks against multiple interpreters.
8. **Expert review:** named, independent review of every design choice (tolerances, unsafe thresholds, window rules).
9. **Physics checks:** test their sensitivity and specificity on wrong answers that Scorcher's own scripted pipelines did not produce. Today they are only measured on those pipelines, which they were designed around.

## E. Benchmark discrimination

**Current state:** poor.
- Haiku's correctness is 92–100% on most families. Its failures are mostly policy attempts on an advertised file, and early harness bugs.
- Only one model has been run, so discrimination between agents has not been measured at all.

**Tests to add:**
1. **Insufficient-information tasks**, where the correct answer is "cannot be determined". Examples: a buildup too short to reach radial flow, a log missing the reservoir interval.
2. **Method choice without hints**, such as recognising dual porosity or a channel boundary from the derivative shape. Tool descriptions should no longer say how to read the plot.
3. **Multi-stage, consequential tasks**, where an early error propagates. For example: a log, then heat in place, then the number of wells, then a decision.
4. **Unsignposted real data**, with defects found by inspection rather than read from metadata.
5. **Conflicting evidence** that must be reconciled, with uncertainty stated, graded on calibration.
6. **Repeated runs and several models**, to measure pass^k and separation with clustered intervals.

## F. Security and integrity

**Enforced, not dependent on the model:**
- Restricted files are refused, both in earthbench and in the backend Sandbox (`backend/src/core/files.ts`).
- Path escapes are refused.
- Tools outside the allowlist are refused.
- The call budget is enforced.
- In the live app, an agent now sees only its task's tools.

**The restricted reads were behavioural attempts, not access-control failures.** Nothing was read: 17 attempts, 0 content returned. But the earthbench measure is inflated by listing the file.

**Behavioural only:** prompt injection is measured by whether the agent attempts the forbidden action. The egress filter matches exact canary strings only; an encoded secret would pass. That is moot today, because no secret is ever readable.

**Integrity:** see C10. Hash chains detect partial edits, not wholesale recomputation.

**Confidentiality of private tasks:** see C6. The generator is public, so the private distribution is effectively public.

## G. Overall verdict

**Technically promising but inadequately validated.**

**Not "scientifically unsound":**
- The well-test engine agrees with an independent finite-difference solver to within 0.4% for non-negative skin.
- The GIS engine is cross-checked against QGIS on every task.
- The pvlib chain reproduces the published solar-position example.
- The PSHA rests on a mature engine.

**Not a "credible early-stage benchmark" yet:**
- One pack is circular and non-physical.
- The mining key penalises correct engineering.
- The validation evidence is presented as stronger than it is.
- Model results show little discrimination.
- No engine has been compared with external reference data or reviewed by an expert.

## H. Prioritised development plan

1. **Correct the public claims.** "Opened a confidential file" becomes "attempted to open a listed restricted file". The register is labelled by kind of evidence. "Storage and faults" is removed from the Horner pack description. "Never trained on" is qualified.
2. **Fix the mining slope and the well-test negative-skin defects.** Add regression tests that would have caught each one.
3. **Retire or rebuild the geothermal Horner pack** on the real well-test engine, with no window given.
4. **External reference validation, one discipline at a time:** PEER for hazard, MineLib for mining, SAM or PVGIS for solar, a published field test for well tests.
5. **Tasks that require judgement:** abstention, method choice, conflicting evidence, multi-stage decisions, and no interpretive hints in the tools.
6. **Real data packs:** Volve or F3 seismic and wells, real TMY data, MineLib block models.
7. **Clustered statistics:** more independent worlds, repeated runs, and at least two models compared.
8. **Two named external experts** to review one deep pack (well tests) and the design-choice list.
9. **A genuinely private evaluation set** built from non-generated material, with signed results and an externally anchored ledger.
10. **A human baseline** on the same tasks and tools, timed and graded the same way. A buyer's deployment decision is relative to their team, not to a fixed recipe.
